# Hiring Strategy Session

The Hiring Strategy Session is the recruiter-led meeting that turns a business need into a
search plan before anything is posted. The Skills on Job Requisitions launch (Workday is
Nike's ATS) changed what "done" looks like for Grade 20+ roles: the meeting now has to end
with an agreed list of 3 to 10 skills in the requisition's Optional Skills field, chosen
from the skills Workday auto-populated from the Job Profile. Everything this skill produces
exists to get the recruiter and the Hiring Manager (HM) to that agreement quickly and with
the same quality every time. The default context is Global Technology (architects,
engineering leaders, technical product and program roles), but the process is the same for
any corporate role.

Read `references/skills-on-reqs-program.md` before advising on anything in Workday. The
rules that most often trip people up:

- Grade 20+ only. Grade 10 and 15 reqs keep their auto-populated skills untouched.
- Optional Skills is the only field you edit. Required Skills is never used; Workday
  blocks the req if anything is entered there. Suggested Skills is a hint list.
- Finalize the list before posting. Do not change aligned skills after posting.
- This is the Crawl phase: skills are not candidate-facing, not standalone assessment
  criteria, and not in interview guides or Workday feedback tasks. Say so to HMs who
  expect otherwise.

Two people use this skill. Work out which one you are helping before doing anything else.

| Mode | Who | What they need |
|---|---|---|
| **Recruiter** | Corporate TA recruiter running the session | Prep checklist, pre-meeting form, HM prep email, facilitation agenda, live note capture, Skills for Success block for Workday, recap email, action tracker |
| **Hiring Manager** | Leader opening the role | A short prep guide, draft answers to the prep questions, a first cut at Skills for Success to react to, plain-language explanation of Connection to Sport and the internal-candidate feedback obligation |

If the person does not say which they are, infer it from context (a recruiter talks about
reqs, Workday, posting strategy, and "my HM"; a hiring manager talks about "my recruiter",
"my team", and the work the role will do). When it is still unclear, ask one short question.

## Workflow

### 1. Establish the stage and the role

Pull the basics from what the person said or pasted: job title, req number if it exists, HM
name, global grade, location, whether the role is new or a backfill, and the business group.
In recruiter mode, ask for anything missing that only the recruiter can supply. In hiring
manager mode, do not ask the HM for grade, req number, or compensation; those are
recruiter-owned, so leave them as `[recruiter to confirm]` and move on. Then establish which
stage the person is at:

- **Before the session** (most common): the user wants to prepare.
- **During the session**: the user wants live prompts and a place to capture decisions.
- **After the session**: the user wants the recap, the Workday skills block, and actions.

Read `references/session-guide.md` for the full three-stage checklist and the question bank.
It is the source of truth for what the session has to cover, so do not improvise core
sections; customize depth and emphasis instead. Check the global grade first: it decides
whether skills alignment is part of this session at all.

### 2. Load the role family

Skills for Success are only useful if they are specific to the work, so pull the matching
role library before drafting anything:

- `references/roles/principal-architect.md` for principal / enterprise / solution / platform
  architects.
- `references/roles/engineering-lead.md` for software engineering managers, directors, and
  staff-plus technical leads.
- `references/roles/technical-product-program.md` for technical product managers and
  technical program managers.
- `references/roles/event-planner.md` and `references/roles/inventory-supply-chain.md` for
  roles outside Global Technology, kept for stretch assignments.
- For any other role, read `references/skills-for-success.md` and build the library from the
  job description and the req's defaulted skills using the same structure.

Each role file gives likely Workday skill labels to look for in the defaulted list or to
search in the Skills Cloud, what each one sounds like when an HM describes it, the sharpest
Team and Role Insight questions for that family, sourcing targets, and the Connection to
Sport angle. They are starting points; the req's own defaulted skills and the HM's input
decide. When an HM names a tool or product as a skill, search the library for the
capability it demonstrates (the library usually has the tool too; prefer the capability
unless the tool is genuinely the job).

### 3. Produce the stage-appropriate deliverables

**Before the session (recruiter mode)**

1. Fill the Strategy Pre-Meeting Form from `assets/pre-meeting-form.md`. Mark every field you
   could not fill as `[CONFIRM IN WORKDAY]` rather than guessing a grade, salary, or req number.
   Wrong numbers in this form end up in offers. Record the grade band (20+ means alignment;
   10 and 15 means no edits) and, for 20+, open the req and list the defaulted Optional
   Skills with a first read of which look broad, duplicated, or off.
2. Draft the HM prep email from `assets/hm-prep-email.md`, sent 24 to 48 hours ahead so the HM
   has time to think about skills before the meeting.
3. Build the session agenda using the A to G structure in `references/session-guide.md`,
   trimmed to the role's complexity. A backfill of a well-understood role needs a 30-minute
   agenda; a new principal architect role needs the full hour.
4. For Grade 20+, draft a **proposed** keep / remove / add view of the defaulted Optional
   Skills, using the role library and the JD, labeled as a draft for the HM to react to.
   Reacting to a marked-up list is far faster for an HM than inventing one, and it keeps the
   result within 3 to 10. If the req has no defaulted skills (Batch B profile), draft the 3
   to 10 from the role library instead and plan to search and add each one.

**Before the session (hiring manager mode)**

Deliver one document, in the order of `assets/hm-prep-package.md`:

1. The one-page prep guide from `assets/hm-prep-guide.md`, personalized to the role.
2. Draft answers to the seven prep questions (they are the numbered list in
   `assets/hm-prep-email.md`) from whatever the HM has told you, with `[brackets]` for gaps
   only they can fill. Keep the HM's own words when they gave them.
3. Connection to Sport in two or three sentences, using the HM-facing wording in
   `references/connection-to-sport.md`. HMs often assume it means "played sports"; it does
   not, and getting this wrong skews the panel's assessment.
4. A first-cut Skills for Success list for them to cut, rank, and add to.

HM-facing documents should not use the session's section letters (A to G) or TA shorthand;
translate "section A questions" into plain topics such as "about the role and the team".

**During the session (either mode)**

Use `assets/session-notes.md` as the capture template. Keep the seven lettered sections in
order, and for each decision write who owns it and by when. The only sections that can be
skipped are those already resolved in prep, and say so in the notes. For Grade 20+, the
skills alignment happens inside section A with the recruiter's screen shared on the req:
frame the purpose, evaluate the defaulted skills with the three guiding questions, then
prioritize and confirm 3 to 10. The suggested language for each move, and for the too-many
and too-few cases, is in `references/skills-on-reqs-program.md`.

**After the session (recruiter mode)**

1. Produce the agreed skills block using the format in `references/skills-for-success.md`:
   the final 3 to 10 Optional Skills as Workday labels, with keep / removed / added shown,
   and a one-line recruiter note per skill on why it matters for this role. The note lives
   in the recruiting plan and the recap, not in Workday. Then the Workday edit: update
   Optional Skills, confirm Required Skills is empty, save before posting.
2. Draft the recap email from `assets/recap-email.md`: decisions, the skills list, interview
   panel and timing, open items with owners and due dates, and the check-in cadence agreed.
   If the session skipped a section the template covers (search targets, Connection to
   Sport behaviors), propose from the role library and mark it `[CONFIRM]` rather than
   leaving it blank; an HM corrects a proposal faster than they fill a blank. Schedule the
   first talent brief on the agreed check-in day, one to two weeks out.
3. Produce the action tracker from `assets/action-tracker.md`. It is recruiter-facing and
   stays out of the email: deliver it as its own section or file so the email can be sent
   as is.

### 4. Quality bar before handing anything back

- Skills: 3 to 10 Optional Skills, each a Workday library label the HM can explain in a
  sentence, with the recruiter's note on why. A list of 15 is a wish list, and the guide is
  explicit that wish lists get cut. Prefer a specific label over a broad one when both exist.
- Skills are one input alongside experience, Leadership Defined, Connection to Sport, and
  demonstrated performance, and in Crawl they are not assessment criteria at all. Never
  write anything that implies interviewers score against the list.
- Grade gate stated. Required Skills empty. Finalized before posting. No post-posting edits.
  A defaulted list that does not fit the role gets aligned anyway and flagged to the
  recruiter's Leader or Peer Coach.
- Internal candidate feedback: for Global Corporate Grade 20+ and Americas Supply Chain 20+,
  remind the HM they must deliver verbal feedback to internal candidates who complete the
  Business Interview, preferably within 3 to 5 business days, and that a Workday task will
  prompt them. Put this in the recap every time it applies.
- Posting: confirm internal-only vs internal/external, duration (minimum one calendar week),
  MYNEXT promotion to retail teammates where the skills translate, and compliance items
  (drug screen, non-compete).
- Never invent compensation, grade, approval, or Workday configuration details. Mark them
  for confirmation. The guide points to the Offer Modeling Tool, Proactive Teaming guidance,
  and TRC; name those sources rather than numbers.

## Output conventions

Return documents in Markdown unless the user asks for a file. When they do, a Word document
or a shared doc is the right fit for anything the HM will read; a Markdown file in the repo is
right for anything the recruiter will keep editing. Keep HM-facing documents short and free
of TA jargon; keep recruiter-facing documents complete.

Use the exact section letters and names from the guide (A Team and Role Insight through G
Interview Reminders) so that notes from different recruiters line up.
