# Role library: Inventory and Supply Chain

Covers inventory management, inventory planning, demand and supply planning, replenishment,
allocation, logistics and distribution operations, and supply chain analytics roles.
Typical context at Nike: Global Service and Places (workplace and corporate services supply
and inventory), Global Operations and Supply Chain, Nike Direct inventory, distribution
center operations.

Note: Americas Supply Chain Grade 20+ roles carry the internal candidate feedback obligation
in section G, the same as Global Corp Grade 20+. Check the grade early.

## Likely Workday skill labels

Look for these on the defaulted list or search them in the Optional Skills field.

- Inventory Management
- Inventory Planning / Replenishment Planning
- Demand Forecasting
- Supply Planning
- Sales and Operations Planning (S&OP)
- Data Analysis
- Process Improvement
- Vendor Management
- Supply Chain Systems (prefer the capability; name the system in your notes)

The capability statements below say what each label sounds like when an HM describes it, and what to listen for. They are recruiter notes, not Workday text.

## What usually makes these searches hard

- "Supply chain" spans planning, sourcing, logistics, and analytics; a candidate strong in
  one is often weak in another. Section A has to name which parts of the chain this role
  actually touches and what systems of record it works in.
- HMs often ask for a specific planning system (SAP, Blue Yonder, o9, etc.). Decide whether
  system fluency is a critical skill or evidence of a broader planning skill; usually it is
  the latter, and treating it as critical shrinks the pool for no gain.
- Analytical depth vs operational judgment: ask which one the last person lacked.

## What the labels mean for this role (recruiter notes for keep / remove / add)

Critical candidates
1. Builds and runs inventory or replenishment plans that balance service level, working
   capital, and cost at the scale of this business.
   Evidence: the plan they owned; the service / inventory outcomes it delivered.
2. Forecasts demand and reconciles it with supply constraints, capacity, and lead times,
   and explains the plan to partners who do not do planning.
   Evidence: forecast accuracy they were measured on; how they handled a miss.
3. Analyzes large operational data sets to find root causes and drive decisions (excess,
   stock-outs, aging, shrink, lead-time variability).
   Evidence: an analysis that changed a decision; the tools they used.
4. Runs the operating cadence (S&OP, weekly planning, exception management) with
   cross-functional partners in merchandising, finance, logistics, and vendors.
   Evidence: a cadence they ran; how escalations were handled.

Supporting candidates
5. Improves processes and systems: identifies a broken step, redesigns it, and gets adoption.
   Evidence: a process they changed and the measured result.
6. Manages vendor and 3PL relationships to performance targets.
   Evidence: a vendor scorecard they ran; a renegotiation.
7. Works fluently in the planning and ERP systems of record and in the analytical tools the
   team uses (name them; this is evidence, not the skill).
   Evidence: systems used in the last two roles.
8. Leads through change: new system, new network, new policy.
   Evidence: an implementation they were part of and their specific role.

Often requested, usually learnable or belongs elsewhere
- A specific planning system: evidence under skill 7.
- Nike product and seasonal calendar knowledge: onboarding plan.
- People management, unless the role has direct reports (confirm on the pre-meeting form).

## Sharpest section A questions for this family

- Which nodes of the chain does this role own: planning, buying, allocation, inbound,
  outbound, returns, or several?
- What are the two metrics this person will be judged on at the end of year one?
- What systems are the source of truth, and how much of the job is fighting those systems?
- Who does this role negotiate with most: merchandising, finance, logistics, or vendors?
- What did the last person do well, and what did the team have to cover for?

## Connection to Sport angle

Low sport fluency requirement, high athlete mindset. Probe for accountability (a miss they
owned), preparation (how they get ready for a peak season), and resilience (a supply shock
and what they did).

## Sourcing targets (section D)

- Multi-channel consumer brands and retailers with large owned inventory and seasonal
  calendars (apparel, footwear, sporting goods, consumer electronics, grocery).
- Logistics and 3PL providers for operations-heavy roles.
- Internal: adjacent planning teams, distribution center leadership, Nike Direct inventory,
  and retail teammates with inventory responsibility via MYNEXT for junior roles.
- Professional associations for supply chain and operations management.
