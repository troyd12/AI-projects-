# Role library: Principal Architect

Covers principal, enterprise, solution, platform, and technology architect roles, and
senior individual-contributor technical leadership roles that set direction across teams.
Typical context at Nike: Global Technology, Global Service and Places (workplace, corporate
services, and facilities technology), supply chain technology, commerce platforms.

## What usually makes these searches hard

- The title means different things in different companies. A "principal architect" at a
  consultancy may be a senior developer; at a large enterprise it may be a director-level
  strategist. The session has to pin down scope: how many teams, how many platforms, what
  decisions this person owns vs recommends.
- HMs often want depth in a specific stack plus enterprise breadth plus executive influence.
  All three rarely exist in one person at the target comp. The cut / rank / add passes in
  `../skills-for-success.md` are where that trade-off gets made explicitly.
- Internal candidates are common and often strong. Section G (internal candidate feedback)
  almost always applies; these roles are typically Global Corp Grade 20+.

## Starter Skills for Success (draft for the HM to cut, rank, add)

Critical candidates
1. Designs end-to-end architectures across multiple systems, teams, and regions, and documents
   them so engineering teams can build from them.
   Evidence: architecture artifacts they authored; a design that shipped at scale.
2. Sets technical direction and standards that other teams adopt without direct authority.
   Evidence: examples of standards or reference architectures adopted beyond their own team.
3. Evaluates build vs buy vs partner options and makes a recommendation an executive can
   decide on, including cost, risk, and sequencing.
   Evidence: a decision record or business case they drove; what happened after.
4. Translates business strategy into a multi-year technology roadmap and sequences it against
   real constraints (budget, legacy, vendor).
   Evidence: roadmap they owned; how it changed when constraints changed.

Supporting candidates
5. Leads architecture review and governance in a way engineers find useful rather than
   bureaucratic.
   Evidence: how they run reviews; what engineers say about them.
6. Deep expertise in the role's primary domain (name it: integration, data, cloud platform,
   workplace / facilities tech, commerce, identity, etc.).
   Evidence: specific systems designed and operated in that domain.
7. Security, resilience, and compliance by design.
   Evidence: how they handled a security or resilience requirement in a design.
8. Mentors senior engineers and grows architectural capability on the team.
   Evidence: people they developed; practices they introduced.

Often requested, usually learnable or belongs elsewhere
- Specific vendor certifications (goes in evidence, not as a skill).
- Nike-specific platform knowledge (onboarding plan).
- Hands-on coding in a particular language (ask whether the role actually codes).

## Sharpest section A questions for this family

- What decisions will this person own outright, and which will they only recommend?
- Who are the five people they must influence to be successful, and what do those people
  care about?
- What is the one architecture problem you would hand them in week one?
- Is this role hands-on at all? If the answer is "sometimes", what does that mean in hours?
- What has the team tried to solve this with before, and why did it not stick?

## Connection to Sport angle

Low sport fluency requirement, high athlete mindset. Probe for preparation (how they get
ready for a major design decision), resilience (a design that was rejected and what they did),
and teamwork (how they bring engineering teams along).

## Sourcing targets (section D)

- Companies running large multi-brand, multi-region consumer platforms (retail, apparel,
  consumer electronics, hospitality).
- Enterprise architecture teams at companies that have recently completed a major platform
  migration in the same domain.
- Internal: current senior and lead engineers, staff engineers, and architects in adjacent
  domains; ask the HM who they already know.
- Professional networks: enterprise architecture and domain-specific communities; the HM's
  own network is often the best source for these roles.
