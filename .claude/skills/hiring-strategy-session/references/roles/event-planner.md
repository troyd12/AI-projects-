# Role library: Event Planner

Covers event planners, event producers, experiential and workplace event roles, and event
operations roles inside corporate services. Typical context at Nike: Global Service and
Places (campus and workplace events, executive and employee experiences), brand and sport
marketing events, partner and athlete events.

## Likely Workday skill labels

Look for these on the defaulted list or search them in the Optional Skills field.

- Event Planning
- Event Management
- Vendor Management / Vendor Negotiation
- Budget Management
- Project Management
- Stakeholder Management
- Logistics Coordination
- Risk Management
- Customer Experience

The capability statements below say what each label sounds like when an HM describes it, and what to listen for. They are recruiter notes, not Workday text.

## What usually makes these searches hard

- The work is visible and the standard is high, so HMs want a portfolio of flawless
  large-scale events. Ask what scale actually recurs (headcount, budget, frequency) so the
  skills list matches the job, not the HM's most memorable event.
- Agency and in-house backgrounds produce different strengths. Agency people are strong on
  production and vendors; in-house people are strong on stakeholders and budgets. Decide
  which matters more in section A.
- Retail athletes often translate well into these roles (operational experience, brand
  passion, consumer instinct). Section A's MYNEXT question is a live one here; decide
  deliberately whether to promote the role to retail teammates.

## What the labels mean for this role (recruiter notes for keep / remove / add)

Critical candidates
1. Plans and executes events end to end at the scale this role recurs at, from brief to
   post-event wrap, on time and on budget.
   Evidence: three recent events with scope, budget, headcount, and outcome.
2. Manages vendors and production partners (venues, AV, catering, staffing, fabrication),
   including negotiating contracts and holding partners to the brief.
   Evidence: how they handled a vendor that underdelivered.
3. Manages budgets with accurate forecasting, change control, and reconciliation.
   Evidence: budget size managed; variance on the last event.
4. Builds and runs an event timeline and run-of-show that cross-functional partners can
   execute without the planner in the room.
   Evidence: a run-of-show they authored; how issues were escalated.

Supporting candidates
5. Partners with senior stakeholders and executives to shape the brief and manage
   expectations when scope and budget collide.
   Evidence: an event where they changed the stakeholder's mind.
6. Designs the attendee experience with the brand in mind, from arrival to departure.
   Evidence: how they decide what an attendee feels at each moment.
7. Operates under live-event pressure: triage, decide, communicate, recover.
   Evidence: the worst thing that went wrong on site and what they did in the next ten minutes.
8. Manages risk, safety, accessibility, and compliance for events, including permits and
   insurance where relevant.
   Evidence: a risk plan they wrote; an incident they prevented.

Often requested, usually learnable or belongs elsewhere
- Specific event-management software (Cvent, etc.): evidence, not a skill.
- Nike campus, venue, and vendor knowledge: onboarding plan.
- Graphic design or content production: ask whether the role actually does this or briefs it.

## Sharpest section A questions for this family

- What are the three events this role owns every year, and what is the budget and headcount
  of each?
- Who signs off on an event, and how far in advance?
- When the last event went wrong, what went wrong, and would this hire have prevented it?
- How much of the role is planning vs on-site production vs stakeholder management, in
  rough percentages?
- Would a retail store or district leader with strong operational experience succeed here?

## Connection to Sport angle

Medium sport fluency (events often carry sport storytelling), high athlete mindset. Probe
for preparation (how they rehearse), adaptability (a live pivot), and teamwork (how they
run a crew they do not manage).

## Sourcing targets (section D)

- In-house event and experience teams at consumer brands with campus or flagship event
  programs (apparel, tech, hospitality, sports leagues and teams).
- Experiential and event production agencies serving those brands.
- Internal: retail teammates via MYNEXT, workplace experience, brand marketing event teams,
  executive assistants who already run large internal events.
- Professional associations for event and meeting professionals; the HM's vendor network
  often knows who is good.
