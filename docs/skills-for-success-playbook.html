Skills for Success Playbook

PLAY 2

Where skills show up in the session

Skills alignment is not a separate meeting. It threads through the strategy session's three stages, and each stage has one skills job.

1

Before

Check the grade. For 20+, open the req, read the defaulted Optional Skills, and mark a first keep / remove / add view. Send the HM prep email 24 to 48 hours ahead.

Skills job: know the list before the HM sees it
2

During

In section A, Team and Role Insight, share your screen on the req. Frame the purpose, evaluate the defaults with three questions, prioritize and confirm 3 to 10.

Skills job: agree the list on screen
3

After

Edit the req: update Optional Skills, confirm Required Skills is empty, save. Put the list in the recap email. Post. Flag any profile concern to your Peer Coach.

Skills job: get it into Workday, then post
PLAY 3

Pick the specific label, not the broad one

Workday skills are short library labels. You do not write them; you choose them. When the defaulted list or the Skills Cloud offers both a broad and a specific label for the same thing, keep the specific one. It says more and hides less.

  • Drop duplicates that mean the same thing for this role.
  • Prefer the capability over the tool unless the tool is the job.
  • Keep one or two interpersonal skills. Senior hires fail on influence more than on depth.

PLAY 4

Every label gets a recruiter note

The label goes in Workday. The note stays with you, in the recruiting plan and the recap email. It records what the hiring manager meant by the label for this role, so that your screen and your talent briefs stay anchored to the conversation.

Notes are not interview criteria in this phase. They are your memory of the alignment.

Label on the requisition

Recruiter note (recap and recruiting plan)
Designs end-to-end architectures across platforms and regions and documents them so engineering teams can build from them. HM's words: "someone whose diagrams ship." Look for architecture artifacts they authored at scale.

PLAY 5

Frame, evaluate, confirm

The three moves from the enablement deck, run with the req on screen. Hiring managers narrow a list far faster than they build one, which is why you always start from the defaults.

  1. Frame the purpose. "We'll review the skills associated with this role and align on those most critical to success."
  2. Evaluate the defaults. Which connect most directly to the outcomes we discussed? Which are too broad, duplicative, or less relevant? Are any critical skills missing?
  3. Prioritize and confirm. Land on 3 to 10. "I'll update the requisition, and you'll see those skills in Workday once it's posted."

Too many: "A longer list makes it harder to see what matters most." Too few: "Let's revisit the key responsibilities and outcomes."

PLAY 6

What the requisition looks like when you are done

Optional Skills holds the final 3 to 10. Removed defaults are gone, added skills came from the Skills Cloud search inside the same field, and Required Skills is empty. Learnable-in-90-days items go to the onboarding plan, not the req.

PLAY 7

Connection to Sport is a behavior, not a biography

It sits beside skills as its own input. Name the two or three athlete mindset behaviors that matter most for the role in the session, so the panel probes for the same things.

"We're looking for evidence of an athlete mindset: how someone prepares, competes, adapts, and works with others, and how that shows up in their work. There's no one right background. It's about behaviors, not participation."

It is

  • Competitive drive
  • Preparation
  • Resilience
  • Accountability
  • Teamwork
  • Adaptability
  • Curiosity

It is not

  • Whether they played sports
  • Being a fan
  • Deep sport knowledge
  • A sports biography question

Technology roles: low need for sport fluency, high need for athlete mindset.
